Editing the internal pathways behind confident OOD mistakes

Unpublished On hold since Oct 2026

Independent research with Prof. Muhammad Tahir · LUMS · January - October 2026

Question. When a classifier confidently accepts an input from a class it has never seen, which internal features carry that mistake, and can removing them help without retraining the model?

What I did. I designed and ran every experiment in this line (more than twenty experiment runs) and wrote the analysis updates for my advisor.

  • Built on the SALVE sparse-autoencoder pipeline, using its official code unmodified, to break a classifier's penultimate features into sparse, inspectable directions.
  • Scored “leakage” pathways: the class-to-feature routes that carry OOD false accepts on a calibration split. I then localized them in images with occlusion maps.
  • Intervened with closed-form rank-one projection edits to the final-layer weights. Edits were chosen on calibration data only and evaluated once on held-out, label-disjoint OOD classes with five standard detectors (MSP, MLS, Energy, ReAct, ViM).

What I found.

  • On label-disjoint Fashion-MNIST open-set splits, mean OOD AUROC rose by roughly 24 to 26 points at less than one point of in-distribution accuracy cost.
  • On CIFAR-10 against CIFAR-100 and on a TinyImageNet subset, gains were real but small (about +0.6 to +0.7 points). A deliberately mismatched Fashion-MNIST split made results worse (-6.4 points), and I report it that way.
  • A pre-registered sweep over 72 Fashion-MNIST splits explained the gap. Edits transfer in proportion to how much of the new failures' pathway mass they cover (Spearman ρ = 0.84; all 24 high-coverage splits improved, 22 of 24 low-coverage splits got worse). The calibration gain itself predicted little (ρ = 0.31).

Status: a CIFAR-100 superclass replication was designed with pre-registered hypotheses but not completed before the project was paused. No write-up has been submitted.

Build-outcome predictors inside CI batching

In progress Manuscript in preparation

Research project, AI-Driven Software Engineering, LUMS · with two classmates · Fall 2026

Question. If a build-outcome predictor scores better on its own, does it also save more continuous-integration runs once it decides which builds a batching workflow skips?

  • We integrated BuildFast, RavenBuild-CR and the Parrot baseline into a prediction-assisted batching simulation and evaluated them on 42 open-source projects from CircleCI and TravisTorrent (4,040 held-out builds).
  • We varied prediction thresholds, timeouts, batch formation and failure diagnosis. Settings were chosen on earlier builds and tested on later, unseen ones.
  • We built a feature-family taxonomy of the 84 predictor features to explain the predictors' behavior.

Draft findings. BuildFast and RavenBuild-CR saved about the same number of executions and made the same decision on 92 to 98% of builds. Both drew 58 to 63% of their decision weight from features that need observed build outcomes. Batching stops observing the builds it skips, so those features freeze; we call this observation starvation. Up to 72% of configurations without a timeout never triggered a build. Batch formation and failure diagnosis mattered far more than the choice of predictor.

FedSCAM: heterogeneity-aware sharpness-aware minimization for federated learning

Course research Preprint on arXiv · not peer-reviewed

Advanced Topics in Machine Learning (graduate), taught by Prof. Muhammad Tahir · September - December 2025

S. Rahil*, Z. A. Ahmad*, T. Asif* (*equal contribution). arXiv:2601.00853 · Code on GitHub

Problem. In federated learning with non-IID clients, sharpness-aware methods use one perturbation radius for every client, which can destabilize the global model when some clients' data is very skewed.

Approach. FedSCAM estimates a heterogeneity score for each client from early-batch gradient norms. It shrinks that client's SAM radius as the score rises, and aggregates updates with weights that penalize heterogeneity and reward alignment with the previous global update.

Results. On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18 under Dirichlet label skew (α = 0.5), FedSCAM improved final accuracy over FedSAM by 4.6 points. On Fashion-MNIST (α = 0.1) it reached 65.41%, against FedLESAM's 65.76%, while taking about 12% less time per round. All nine baselines were re-implemented under identical splits. The study used two to three seeds without reported variance, which the preprint notes as a limitation.

My part. Equal contribution, and I am the first-listed author. I worked on the algorithm design (the per-client radius and the heterogeneity- and alignment-aware aggregation), ran the Fashion-MNIST comparison against the sharpness-aware baselines and the ablation and hyperparameter studies, and wrote most of the manuscript.

Course research reports

  1. Inductive Bias in Vision: CNNs vs. ViTs, VAEs vs. GANs, and CLIP, compared through out-of-distribution generalization.

  4. Skewed Clients, Skewed Outcomes: how label heterogeneity shapes federated averaging.

  6. Training Values, Finding Circuits: an RLHF stack under an 8-bit + LoRA budget, and grokking on modular addition dissected with Fourier analysis.
