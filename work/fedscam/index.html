FedSCAM: heterogeneity-aware sharpness-aware minimization for federated learning

A per-client perturbation radius and alignment-aware aggregation that keep sharpness-aware training stable when client data is badly skewed.

Advanced Topics in Machine Learning (graduate), Prof. Muhammad Tahir

arXiv preprintFirst-listed author

+4.6 points over FedSAM on CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18 at α = 0.5 (58.00% to 62.60%)

The problem

Sharpness-aware minimization (SAM) looks for flat minima by minimizing the worst-case loss in a small ball around the weights:

minw max‖ε‖₂ ≤ ρ L(w + ε)

FedSAM runs this on every client and averages the results, with one global radius ρ. Under label skew, clients differ a lot in gradient scale, noise and local curvature. A single ρ over-perturbs unstable clients, which amplifies client drift, and under-uses the stable ones.

How FedSCAM works

Each round adds four light steps to FedSAM, all computed from signals the clients already have.

  1. Heterogeneity score. Each client averages its gradient norm over its first B = 3 batches: hi ≈ (1/B) Σb ‖∇L(wt; Bi,b)‖₂.
  2. Alignment. A one-batch pilot direction is compressed by random projection to 256 or 512 dimensions and compared with the last global update, ci = cos(si, ut-1). Clients pointing the same way as the federation get credit: hiadj = hi · max(0, 1 - κci).
  3. Adaptive radius. Each client runs SAM with its own radius ρi = ρmax / (1 + αρ hiadj), with ρmax = 0.05. It works as a trust throttle: noisy clients get a tighter trust region and stable ones can explore.
  4. Weighted aggregation. Instead of weighting by sample count alone, the server uses Si ∝ Ni · 1/(1 + γhiadj) · max(0, 1 + βci). An optional step clusters update summaries into K groups and downweights the smaller of any two conflicting (negative-cosine) updates by a factor λ.

Experimental setup

  • Data. CIFAR-10 and Fashion-MNIST split across 10 clients with full participation, using Dirichlet label skew Dir(α) with α = 0.1 (extreme), 0.5 (moderate) and 1.0 (mild), and at least 10 samples per client.
  • Models. ResNet-18 (four residual stages, 64 to 512 channels) and a small CNN for fast ablations.
  • Training. SGD with a learning rate of 0.01. The Fashion-MNIST study ran 10 rounds of 5 local epochs at batch size 256, and the CIFAR-10 comparison ran 15 rounds.
  • Baselines. SAM family: FedSAM, FedLESAM and FedWMSAM. Aggregation family: FedAvg, uniform averaging, FedAvgM, q-FedAvg, FedProx, FedLW and FedNoLoWe. A hybrid, FedLWSAM, and three FedSCAM ablations: aggregation only (WA), radius only (SAM) and the full method. All ran on identical splits and hyperparameters.

Results on Fashion-MNIST (α = 0.1)

Under extreme skew, FedSCAM reached 65.41% after 10 rounds, within 0.35 points of FedLESAM (65.76%) and well ahead of the loss-weighting and momentum baselines. FedLESAM needed about 20 seconds more per round, so FedSCAM reached 99.4% of its accuracy while running about 12% faster.

Results on CIFAR-10

Across skew levels the picture is more mixed, so here it is in full. The aggregation-only variant, FedSCAM (WA), was the best method at moderate skew (70.63% at α = 0.5, ahead of FedAvg at 69.68% and FedLW at 69.42%). That suggests filtering conflicting updates matters more there than changing the local optimizer. At α = 0.1, plain FedAvg was still best.

In longer runs at α = 0.5, the full method beat FedSAM by 2.81 points with the small CNN (56.57% to 59.38%, 30 rounds) and by 4.60 points with ResNet-18 (58.00% to 62.60%, 50 rounds, 1 local epoch).

Stability and client drift

Server momentum broke down under skew: FedAvgM collapsed to random guessing (about 10% accuracy) for several consecutive rounds before recovering, while FedSCAM (WA) converged steadily. FedSCAM avoids this because its alignment check is computed fresh each round instead of accumulating stale history.

We measured client drift as the mean L2 distance between each local model and the global model, dt = (1/|St|) Σi ‖wi - wt‖₂. FedSAM kept drifting far more than FedAvg throughout. After a large first round, FedSCAM settled close to FedAvg and stayed there.

Sensitivity

  • Maximum radius ρmax of 0.01, 0.05 or 0.1. 0.1 gave the highest final accuracy, so the throttle tolerates radii that usually destabilize SAM. 0.01 was too conservative.
  • Heterogeneity penalty γ of 0, 1 or 5. Any γ above 0 beat γ = 0, and γ = 1 gave the steadiest gain.
  • Alignment boost β of 0, 0.8 or 2. Rewarding aligned clients did not help in this setting, so detecting conflict mattered more than rewarding agreement.

Limitations

Results come from two to three seeds without reported variance, which the preprint notes. Computing hi and the pilot directions adds overhead that may matter on very small edge devices, and the clustering step (K and λ) is tuned by hand. Next steps include cheaper proxies for heterogeneity and convergence bounds for alignment-aware weighting in non-convex federated optimization.

My part

Equal contribution, and I am the first-listed author. I worked on the algorithm design (the per-client radius and the heterogeneity- and alignment-aware aggregation), ran the Fashion-MNIST comparison against the sharpness-aware baselines and the ablation and hyperparameter studies, and wrote most of the manuscript.
